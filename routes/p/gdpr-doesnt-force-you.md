# GDPR doesn't force you...

...to add a cookie banner to your website.

If you don't track personally identifiable information, you
don't need to disclose anything. You only have to have a consent
banner if you track PII. The banner must have a 1 click opt out.
That's it.

I've seen a lot of supposedly technically literate people blaming
GDPR for cookie banners. They should blame the people who want to
track you, not GDPR. If anything, GDPR makes it easier for people to
reject cookies because it standardizes the cookie banner rejection
button. In fact, there are browser extensions that auto reject
cookie banners and the GDPR compliant ones are easier to automate.

Now there's a lot of people who don't care about being tracked, and
that's okay. It's unfortunate that the law forces these people to
see a banner if the website wants their data. You might have philosophical
debates about whether the legislation of mandatory consent banners
are worth it or not. Every law has a trade off. You might also argue
about whether the law mandating health warning labels on cigarette
cartons are worth it. But I'd just like to point out that some people
have thought long and hard about these trade offs. Probably for much
longer and harder than you have. And they've decided that it's worth
it. These people were elected by the people of Europe. Now you may also
object...*but Dhruv, we're not Europeans. Why must we live under these
continental tyrranies?*
Well to that I say is blame the company who forces you to deal with
Europe specific regulations because they didn't think worth it to
disable it for your country.
